# Founder Intelligence: Lens Library

Each lens is a way of looking at one decision. Pick 2-4 that fit the decision, run them, then find where they disagree. Don't average them.

- **Core lenses (9):** the default set. `scripts/lens-router.mjs` routes to these.
- **Specialist lenses (6):** pull one in when the decision clearly matches its "Use when." In `deep` mode, check all six for fit.

Each entry: what it optimizes for, the core question, signature principles, and when to use it.

---

## Core Lenses

### Buffett/Munger Capital Discipline Lens
*Optimizes for: Return on capital, durable moats, simplicity, predictability*

Core Question: Does this business earn returns because it's truly better, or because conditions are temporarily favorable?

Signature Principles:
- Pricing power is the tell.
- Avoid complexity that hides weak economics.
- Opportunity cost is always present.
- Invert: list what would guarantee failure, then avoid it.
- Time rewards the truly good business.

Use when: Evaluating durability, acquisitions, expansion, pricing, or any "great story" with unclear economics.

---

### Bezos Flywheel Lens
*Optimizes for: Customer trust first, scale economies later, reinvestment over extraction*

Core Question: If we invest aggressively now, will this create a self-reinforcing flywheel later?

Signature Principles:
- Work backwards from the customer, not forward from your capabilities.
- Invest in what won't change for customers.
- Build capabilities before monetizing them.
- Separate one-way doors (slow down) from two-way doors (move fast).
- Accept short-term inefficiency for long-term dominance.

Use when: Platform dynamics, scale unlocks advantage, you can reinvest, or the team is debating a reversible decision like it's permanent.

---

### Jobs Simplicity Lens
*Optimizes for: Focus, product taste, saying no*

Core Question: If we could only do one thing, what would it be, and what are we willing to kill to do it well?

Signature Principles:
- Focus means saying no to good ideas.
- The product is the strategy. Everything else supports it.
- Simplicity is the result of hard decisions, not the absence of features.
- Own the whole experience where it matters to the customer.

Use when: Roadmap sprawl, too many offers, a product that does everything adequately and nothing memorably.

---

### Drucker Purpose Lens
*Optimizes for: Clarity on what business you're actually in, customer definition, effectiveness over efficiency*

Core Question: What business are we really in, who is the customer, and what does that customer value?

Signature Principles:
- The purpose of a business is to create a customer.
- Doing the wrong thing efficiently is still wrong.
- Results exist outside the company. Inside are only costs.
- Plan abandonment: what would we not start today if we weren't already doing it?

Use when: Positioning confusion, a business that has drifted, or a choice between serving two different customers.

---

### Godin Smallest Viable Audience Lens
*Optimizes for: Specificity, permission, being remarkable to a few*

Core Question: Who is this for, and who is it not for?

Signature Principles:
- Pick the smallest audience you can serve brilliantly and still sustain the business.
- Remarkable means worth making a remark about.
- Marketing is a promise to a specific group of people.
- Trust and permission compound; attention bought once has to be bought again.

Use when: Messaging that tries to appeal to everyone, a launch with no clear first audience, niche-down decisions.

---

### Horowitz Wartime/Peacetime Lens
*Optimizes for: Execution under pressure, leadership clarity, hard calls made fast*

Core Question: Is the company in wartime (an existential threat) or peacetime (an expanding market), and are we acting like it?

Signature Principles:
- There is no recipe for hard things. Make the call and own it.
- Wartime needs directness and speed; peacetime needs room for experiments.
- Take care of the people, the products and the profits, in that order.
- Bad news travels slowly unless leaders pull it up.

Use when: Layoffs, a cash crunch, a team in conflict, an urgent competitive threat, a hire or fire under pressure.

---

### Thiel Non-Consensus Lens
*Optimizes for: Monopoly logic, secrets, escaping competition*

Core Question: What important truth do we believe that very few people agree with, and does this decision build on it?

Signature Principles:
- Competition is for losers. Aim for a category you can dominate.
- Start with a small market and own it before expanding.
- A secret is something true that isn't widely known yet.
- Last-mover advantage beats first-mover: be the one that locks in the category.

Use when: Market entry, category creation, deciding whether to compete head-on or go around.

---

### Dalio Principles Lens
*Optimizes for: Repeatable decision systems, radical transparency, learning from mistakes*

Core Question: Is this a one-off decision, or a type we'll face again, and do we have a principle for it?

Signature Principles:
- Pain plus reflection equals progress.
- Turn recurring decisions into written principles.
- Weigh opinions by track record (believability).
- Diagnose root causes before designing fixes.

Use when: The same mistake keeps recurring, team decisions depend on who's in the room, or you're building a process.

---

### Helmer 7 Powers Lens
*Optimizes for: Structural advantage, replication difficulty, economic capture*

Core Question: What specifically prevents competitors from copying this?

The 7 Powers: Scale economies, Network effects, Switching costs, Cornered resources, Process power, Branding power, Counter-positioning.

If none exist, the advantage is fragile.

Use when: Validating "defensibility" claims, pricing decisions, and deciding where to invest for durable advantage.

## Specialist Lenses

### Trader Joe's Lens
*Optimizes for: Differentiation, operational simplicity, customer trust via curation, economic density*

Core Question: Are we deliberately different in a way that improves economics, or copying industry norms?

Signature Principles:
- Assortment is a cost center. Curation builds trust faster than choice.
- Operational model is strategy, not support.
- Economic density beats revenue scale.
- Refuse participation in industry norms.

Use when: Crowded markets, feature parity, margin pressure, complexity creep.

---

### Porter Competitive Strategy Lens
*Optimizes for: Tradeoffs, defensible positioning, category structure*

Core Question: What are we choosing NOT to do?

Signature Principles:
- Strategy is a different set of activities, not better execution of the same ones.
- Operational excellence is not strategy.
- Fit between activities creates defensibility.

Use when: Everyone claims differentiation. Stuck in feature parity.

---

### Howard Marks Risk Lens
*Optimizes for: Survival, asymmetric risk awareness, second-order thinking*

Core Question: What happens if we are wrong?

Signature Principles:
- Avoiding ruin beats maximizing gain.
- Risk is hidden in good times.
- Consensus is a risk factor.
- Cycles always exist.

Use when: Momentum is high, everyone agrees, leverage is rising.

---

### Ferriss Small Bets Lens
*Optimizes for: Learning speed, reversibility, optionality*

Core Question: What is the smallest action that gives real signal?

Signature Principles:
- Prototype before deciding.
- Fear is data.
- Test before scaling.

Use when: You're stuck deciding. Downside feels abstract.

---

### Red Bull Cultural Dominance Lens
*Optimizes for: Cultural integration, emotional positioning, message discipline*

Core Question: Are we selling a product, or engineering belief?

Signature Principles:
- Marketing can be the core asset.
- Create the category you want to lead.
- Use distinctiveness to avoid commoditization.

Use when: Adoption depends on a shift in perception. Product benefits are hard to explain functionally.

---

### Watkins Execution Lens
*Optimizes for: Early wins, alignment, credibility loops*

Core Question: Where can we create momentum fastest?

Signature Principles:
- Diagnose before acting.
- Secure visible early wins.
- Build coalitions intentionally.
- Translate strategy into responsibilities and metrics.

Use when: Entering a new role, organization is stalled, execution is noisy.

---

## Guardrails (Always Active)

- Do not give motivational advice without decision context.
- Do not collapse lenses into generic consensus.
- Do not use quotes as authority.
- Do not recommend action without naming tradeoffs.
- Do not assume scale is good.
- Do not flatten a nuanced recommendation into "it depends."
- Always ask: what structural problem are we solving?

Tone: Calm. Direct. No hype. Clarity over completeness. Prefer subtraction before addition.
